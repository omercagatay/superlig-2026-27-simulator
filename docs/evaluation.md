# Evaluation record

What each evaluation tool in this repository measures, every result it has produced with its interval, the tuning history and the negative results. It is the maintainers' full record: [`model-card.md`](model-card.md) is the summary written for readers, and [`preregistration-2026-27.md`](preregistration-2026-27.md) fixes what the live season is used to test.

The sections were moved here from `CLAUDE.md` in November 2026 with their wording unchanged, apart from pointers back to it. `CLAUDE.md` keeps the rules a change has to follow; this file keeps what was measured. Figures in metric markers are checked against `data/metrics/` in CI; every other figure says what produced it.

## Walk-forward backtest (`examples/backtest.rs`)

The yardstick for every model change. It replays seasons 2016-17..2023-24 (development, <!-- metric:dev_n:,d -->2,717<!-- /metric --> matches) match day by match day: each match is priced with the production rules (`sim::elo_delta`, `elo_lambdas`, `blend_lambdas`, `probs_from_lambdas`, `preseason_ratings_with`) using only earlier dates, and same-day results are revealed only after pricing. Its summary gives each aspect's mean loss (`dev_*`: 1X2 log-loss, RPS, over/under 2.5, BTTS and exact-score log-loss), then skill against a base-rate forecast whose rates come only from the seasons before the one scored (`*_skill`, `*_rpss`, the base rate's own loss in `*_base`), and calibration per outcome: mean forecast against observed rate, and the slope of a logistic recalibration with its standard error (`*_cal_*`; 1 is calibrated, below 1 the forecasts spread too wide), then calibration in the large: mean λ total, P(3+ goals) and P(both score) against what happened (`*_goals_*`, `*_ou25_pred`/`_obs`, `*_btts_pred`/`_obs`). Each season's line adds its fitted Dixon-Coles ρ and its mean λ total against goals scored. `EVAL_HOLDOUT=1` adds 2024-25 and 2025-26 (`hold_*`, <!-- metric:hold_n:d -->647<!-- /metric --> matches); `EVAL_SEASON_SIM=N` replays each season with the production simulator and compares the spread of final points (it refuses to run with a λ knob set, `EVAL_HA`, `EVAL_DDIV`, `EVAL_BASE`, `EVAL_GOALS`, `EVAL_RHO` or the lift's, since its `World` prices with production's rules and would ignore them); `EVAL_DUMP=file.csv` writes per-match rows; `EVAL_METRICS=file.json` writes the summary as one sorted JSON object. `EVAL_*` knobs (documented in the file header) override constants for screening, `EVAL_PIVOT`/`EVAL_LIFT`/`EVAL_FOLLOW` the underdog lift (`EVAL_LIFT=0` turns it off), `EVAL_GOALS` scales both λs after it, `EVAL_RHO` sets the score table's ρ (a number, or `fit` for the season's fitted ρ even at a Dixon-Coles weight of 0); with none set it calls the production functions and runs exactly what ships.

**The October 2026 tuning, against its noise.** It raised in-season `ELO_K` 20→60 with `D_DIV` 1600→1200 and added the underdog lift. `EVAL_K=20 EVAL_DDIV=1600 EVAL_LIFT=0` is the model before it (its preseason track already ran at K 20, today's `PRESEASON_K`). To reproduce: run the backtest with `EVAL_HOLDOUT=1 EVAL_DUMP=pre.csv` and those knobs, again with `EVAL_DUMP=prod.csv` and none, then `cargo run --release --example compare -- pre.csv prod.csv`. `compare` pairs the matches and gives each aspect's mean difference with a 95% date-block bootstrap interval (10,000 resamples; a date's matches move together because they are priced from one rating state), a Diebold-Mariano-style t and, last, a 95% season-cluster t interval (cluster-robust standard error over seasons, Student's t with seasons − 1 degrees of freedom: a season's scoring level moves all its matches together, which date blocks cannot see; with the two holdout seasons it has one degree of freedom and is very wide). On the current history (differences prod − pre; these come from `compare`, are not in a metrics file and are not gated):
- Dev: every aspect improves, significantly for 1X2 (−0.0077, [−0.0122, −0.0031], t −3.24), RPS (t −3.09), BTTS (t −3.15) and exact score (t −2.92); over/under 2.5 is borderline (t −1.80, interval reaching +0.0003).
- Holdout: 1X2 −0.0066 [−0.0170, +0.0043], t −1.21, not significant; RPS, over/under and exact score are not either. Only BTTS is (−0.0044 [−0.0081, −0.0008], t −2.36).
- The two changes apart (`EVAL_LIFT=0` alone against prod, and against pre): K/`D_DIV` carries the 1X2 and RPS gain (dev 1X2 −0.0076, t −3.23) but makes holdout BTTS worse (+0.0028, t +3.17). The lift does nothing for 1X2 or RPS (holdout 1X2 +0.0008, t +1.00) and is the BTTS lever (dev −0.0018, t −2.53; holdout −0.0072, t −2.87).

These intervals ignore model selection. The dev seasons chose the tuning, so their intervals are optimistic, and the holdout informed two decisions in that run, so treat it as lightly used; the live 2026-27 season is the clean test. The tuning's stated rationale was that most of the gap to closing odds opens after matchday 10, so in-season learning was the lever and the preseason prior is not where the error is. Nothing in the repo sourced that claim, and the market benchmark (below) neither confirms nor refutes it: against pre-match odds, not closing ones, the gap after matchday 10 minus the gap in matchdays 1-10 is <!-- metric:market:dev_md_late_minus_early_1x2_ll:+.4f -->+0.0106<!-- /metric --> on dev ([−0.00221, +0.02350]) and <!-- metric:market:hold_md_late_minus_early_1x2_ll:+.4f -->-0.0100<!-- /metric --> on the holdout ([−0.04269, +0.02268]), separating neither set. Do not cite it as a finding. Tried and rejected (do not re-run without a new idea): promoted-club priors, regressing or widening preseason ratings, weekly in-season Dixon-Coles/pi refits (redundant once Elo is fast), early-season K schedules, phase-dependent blend weights, log/linear/no margin multipliers, goal-difference Elo (much worse), geometric blending, model-driven goal totals, pi-rating learning-rate changes, a closeness-dependent draw boost (won on dev, failed the holdout), and the phase-4 sweep below.

The tuning run itself was measured on the history as it then was, which lacked 27 played 2024-25 matches (its point estimates: dev 1.0068 → 0.9991, holdout 0.9805 → 0.9759). On the corrected history (2024-25 complete bar its award, Karagümrük under one name) dev is unchanged, since no dev season changed, and the pre-tuning model scores 0.9822 on the holdout. 2025-26 is 0.0019 worse than before, nearly all of it in Karagümrük's 34 matches: the corrected entry rating (about 1504 instead of 1450) suited a side that finished last less well.

**The phase-4 match sweep (October 2026) shipped nothing.** 202 dev configurations: `EVAL_GOALS` 0.92-1.02, `EVAL_BASE` 1.25-1.35, `EVAL_RHO` −0.18..0, the whole weight simplex at step 0.1, and joint refinements. The grid and its nested selection are one-off results of the phase-4 session: each configuration can be re-run with the `EVAL_*` knobs, but the grid and the selection were scripted outside the repo and cannot be re-run from it. A reviewer rebuilt a 132-configuration subset (the weight simplex × {fitted ρ, ρ −0.10}) and got the same best setting and conclusion (nested +0.00056, season t +0.70). These come from the sweep and `compare`, not a metrics file. What it found:
- Goal level: dev λ totals run <!-- metric:dev_goals_pred:.3f -->2.865<!-- /metric --> a match against <!-- metric:dev_goals_obs:.3f -->2.816<!-- /metric --> scored, but the per-season gap has an SD of 0.135, so the dev mean gap (0.049) is about one season-clustered standard error (0.0455, the cluster-robust one `compare` uses). `BASE` 1.30 or a 0.98 goal scale closes it and helps over/under 2.5 and exact score slightly, but costs BTTS and does not hold in leave-one-season-out selection on dev. ## Season-level evaluation (`examples/season_eval.rs`)

The backtest scores single matches; this scores what the site headlines: finishing positions, title, top four, relegation and final points. For every season (dev 2016-17..2023-24; `EVAL_HOLDOUT=1` adds the two holdout seasons) and three snapshots (`s0` nothing played, `s33` and `s67` a third and two thirds of the fixtures played, cut on a date) it rebuilds the `World` production would have held on that date (`preseason_ratings_with` over earlier results, the in-season Elo walk through `refresh_ratings`, Dixon-Coles and pi fit on earlier results and frozen, the shipped blend) and plays the rest of the season <!-- metric:season:n_sims:,d -->2,000<!-- /metric --> times with `World::simulate_one_dispersed` under `Dispersion::PRODUCTION`, unless the dispersion knobs say otherwise. Outcomes are the official tables, awards counted (`SEASON_AWARDS=drop` scores played football only); relegation places come from the data, with the 2019-20 and 2022-23 exceptions named and checked. It scores position RPS, Brier, log-loss and Brier skill (against the base rate places/clubs) for title, top four and relegation, the probability given to the champion, randomized PIT and 50%/80% interval coverage of final points, the forecast SD of points against the realized error, the CRPS of final points (`pts_crps`, in points: a proper score of the whole points distribution, so it rewards spread only where the errors need it, which coverage and PIT cannot tell), and simulated against actual table spread. Pooled figures carry 95% season-cluster bootstrap intervals: <!-- metric:season:dev_n_seasons -->8<!-- /metric --> dev seasons make them rough, and with two holdout seasons an interval shows only how far apart they are. Knobs and metric definitions are in the file header. The dispersion knobs (`SEASON_SIGMA`, `SEASON_SHOCK=elo|blend`, `SEASON_HOT_K`, `SEASON_DRIFT`; see "Dispersion" under "Simulation core" in CLAUDE.md) default to production's, and at their defaults the run is production's draw for draw. `SEASON_DUMP=file.csv` writes one row per club, season and snapshot; `compare --season A.csv B.csv` pairs two dumps and gives B − A per snapshot and pooled for position RPS, points CRPS and the title, top-four and relegation Brier scores, with a season-cluster bootstrap interval, a season-cluster t interval (read this one: a percentile bootstrap over 8 clusters is anti-conservative) and how many seasons B is better in. `SEASON_METRICS=file.json` writes the pooled point estimates (`{dev,hold}_{s0,s33,s67}_{metric}`); the committed copy is diffed in CI (about 5 s on 4 cores; the output does not depend on the core count). The intervals are only printed, so the ones quoted here are not gated.

**Phase 4 (October 2026) tried to widen the seasons; production kept its shock.** Match-level calibration is close (no slope distinguishable from 1 in the backtest) while the season spread is too narrow, so phase 4 looked for uncertainty that accumulates over a season. A sweep of 47+ dispersions on dev, 5,000 seasons per snapshot (differences against production; figures from `season_eval` and `compare --season`, not gated):
- Hot simulation is best by points CRPS: K 60 with σ 75 gives pooled CRPS −0.075, position RPS −0.0020, 80% coverage 81.6/80.3/80.9%. About half of that gain is a shift of the mean forecast, not better spread: Elo's logistic expectancy disagrees with the blend's λ, so in-trial updates pull Elo towards the blend's view, moving mean points by RMS 0.8-1.1 at s0 and s33. A zero-mean hot update does no better than a plain shock. The zero-mean variant and this split of the gain are one-off results of the phase-4 session, from code that is not in the repo, and cannot be re-run from it; `SEASON_HOT_K` runs the plain hot update.
- Drift (a weekly random walk) is dominated by hot simulation.
- The whole-model shock (`SEASON_SHOCK=blend`, σ 75) fixes dev: 80% coverage 82.9/79.6/79.6%, 50% coverage 55.3/50.0/54.6%, PIT SD 0.276/0.292/0.283, error ratio 0.956/1.050/1.030. Pooled position RPS −0.00162 (bootstrap [−0.00279, −0.00044], season t(7) [−0.00315, −0.00009], better in 7 of 8 seasons), pooled points CRPS −0.0325 (bootstrap [−0.084, +0.019], t [−0.099, +0.034]), better in only 5 of 8 seasons, with both intervals spanning 0. At s67 it was no better on dev: points CRPS better in 2 of 8 seasons, position RPS in 3 of 8. σ 50/75/100 give pooled CRPS −0.022/−0.033/+0.013. These dev intervals ignore the selection among the 47+ settings.

The holdout rule was written down in the session notes before the holdout run, but not committed at the time: the commits that record it came after the result. It said: adopt the whole-model shock unless pooled points CRPS or pooled position RPS is worse on the holdout. Both were (5,000 seasons, seed 12345, `compare --season`): points CRPS +0.064, better in 0 of 2 seasons, and position RPS +0.00039. The position-RPS difference is tiny and seed-sensitive (+0.00039 at seed 12345, +0.00011 at 999, +0.00042 at 4242; bootstrap [−0.00124, +0.00211], better in 1 of 2 seasons); the CRPS leg decides (+0.058 to +0.064 across those seeds, better in 0 of 2 seasons). At s33 it was worse in both seasons on both scores; at s67 position RPS was worse in both and points CRPS in one (2024-25 +0.040, 2025-26 −0.007). Production was not over-confident at either snapshot, and the shock made it too wide (error ratio 0.663 at s33 and 0.740 at s67). s0 was mixed (relegation Brier −0.0105, title +0.0028). So production keeps the σ 75 Elo shock; `ShockOn::Blend`, hot updates and drift stay as knobs. Two holdout seasons settle little, and `RATING_SIGMA` was itself chosen on their table spread, so this is a veto, not proof that the dev finding is wrong. The live 2026-27 season is the clean test, but scoring it is a follow-up, not a re-run: `season_eval` scores only seasons in `data/superlig_results.csv`, which runs through 2025-26, and takes a season's relegation places from the next season's clubs. It needs 2026-27's rows in the history (`fetch_history.py` extended to fetch them) and that season's relegation places. Judge any further change by pooled `pts_crps` and position RPS from `compare --season`, the decision scores, alongside the backtest's match-level log-loss; coverage, PIT and error ratio are diagnostics. Decide the holdout rule, and commit it, before running the holdout.

## Market benchmark (`examples/market.rs`, `scripts/fetch_odds.py`)

Offline evaluation only, under "No licensed betting sources" in CLAUDE.md: no bookmaker odds or market figure may reach the app, its API or its UI, and nothing in `src/` or `frontend/` reads the odds. `scripts/fetch_odds.py` (standard library; it imports `fetch_history`'s download, alias, date-skew and digest helpers) takes the MIT archive's Süper Lig odds, which the archive documents as pre-match (it has no closing prices): Bet365 1X2 (`OddHome/OddDraw/OddAway`), the best price of ~17 books (`Max*`), and both for over/under 2.5. It reconciles every 2016-17..2025-26 row with `data/superlig_results.csv` (same ordered pair and score, dates at most a day apart, so 2025-26 is checked against the TFF-sourced rows; on 2026-10-19 no date differed), drops the 29 earthquake walkovers, allows a history match without odds only in the archive's 2024-25 gap (`ARCHIVE_GAP`), and pins the archive slice by `ODDS_SLICE_SHA256`. 2026-27 rows are reconciled with the calendar's played results (`LIVE_ALIASES` adds Çorum) into a file of their own, not pinned. Run `scripts/fetch_fixtures.py` first: an archive 2026-27 row whose fixture the committed calendar has not played yet is skipped, reported on stderr and counted (`examples/market.rs`'s live join does the same, `live_unplayed`), so the live comparison is complete only once the calendar has caught up; a score that disagrees with a recorded result, or a pair that is no fixture, still stops the run. Both files go to `data/odds/`, which `.gitignore` excludes. Coverage on 2026-10-19: every dev season has complete odds bar three 2017-18 Bet365 1X2 prices (<!-- metric:market:dev_n:,d -->2,714<!-- /metric --> of 2,717), the holdout <!-- metric:market:hold_n:,d -->620<!-- /metric --> of 647 (2024-25 314 of 341: the gap). Best-price 1X2 books come out at or below 1 (a stale price at one of the books) in <!-- metric:market:dev_max_book_le1_1x2:,d -->580<!-- /metric --> dev matches (21.4%) and <!-- metric:market:hold_max_book_le1_1x2:,d -->26<!-- /metric --> holdout ones (4.2%), falling from 44.1% in 2016-17 to 0.7% in 2025-26 (printed per season, not gated; over/under books <!-- metric:market:dev_max_book_le1_ou:,d -->126<!-- /metric --> and <!-- metric:market:hold_max_book_le1_ou:,d -->4<!-- /metric -->), so Bet365 is the primary line and the best price only a check.

`examples/market.rs` joins a backtest `EVAL_DUMP` (with `EVAL_HOLDOUT=1`) with those files on season, date and pair (scores must agree; at most `MAX_LEFT_OUT`, 5% of a set, may lack odds), removes the margin with Shin's method (the primary, chosen before the run and written in the session notes but, like the phase-4 holdout rule, committed together with the results; proportional normalization and the best-price line are the checks) and reports model − market per set with `compare`'s date-block bootstrap, DM t and season-cluster t. That code now sits in `examples/common/stats.rs`, which both include with `#[path]`; `compare`'s output is byte-identical. ## Model selection (`examples/arena.rs`)

The 0.5/0.3/0.2 ensemble weights are validated by backtest, not guessed: the arena fits every candidate (nine fitted models and three baselines: home-Poisson, base rates and uniform) on past seasons and scores them on held-out 2024-25 (validation) and 2025-26 (test). The production blend wins neither season (test 1.0011, third behind Dixon-Coles 1.5y at 0.9994 and `logit-stack` at 1.0003; validation 0.9828, behind the validation-fitted weights at 0.9737), but each season's leader does worse than it on the other (Dixon-Coles 1.5y and `logit-stack` 0.9978 on validation; the fitted weights 1.0051 on test), and weights grid-fitted to the validation season overfit it. Re-run the arena before changing `ENSEMBLE_WEIGHTS` defaults. ML has not earned a place: the walk-forward logistic stacker (in the arena, `logit-stack`) edges the blend on the 2025-26 test season by 0.0008 (it trailed by 0.0003 before the 2024-25 gap was filled) but trails it by 0.0150 on validation, and sklearn GBM/RF/MLP (side experiment, `ARENA_DUMP_DIR=<dir>` dumps the feature CSV; not re-run on the corrected history) scored behind the ensemble on 2025-26 — results-only features don't give ML room to win here. The arena freezes every rating for a whole season and its Elo is its own reimplementation; `examples/backtest.rs` supersedes it for anything production-facing.

## Season dispersion (`examples/arena.rs`)

The arena also asks whether replayed tables spread as wide as the real played-match tables. With no shared season shock the 2024-25/2025-26 standard deviations are 13.4/13.0 versus 17.3/15.2 observed; `data::RATING_SIGMA` (75, a row of the arena's sweep) raises them to 15.0/14.4. The arena's replay applies that draw its own way: it tilts each match's win/loss log-odds by ln 10 / 400 per Elo point of the drawn gap, draws held, slightly weaker per point than a shock on the whole model's λ. Production adds the shock to the Elo ratings before `elo_lambdas`, where it reaches λ only through the Elo component's blend weight and the underdog lift compresses low λs further. So 75 is the arena's figure carried over to a different mechanism, not one fitted to production's simulator; `season_eval` is the check of what production simulates (see "Season-level evaluation"). The 2024-25 history leaves out its one administrative award (Galatasaray v Adana Demirspor, awarded 3-0, never finished), so the diagnostic compares played-fixture points on both sides. `fixture_probs` still uses the point estimate, leaving per-match calibration untouched.

Residual known limitation: even at the calibrated sigma, the validation season's 92-point played-match leader (Galatasaray) is only about a 7% event (6.7%) in the arena's frozen-rating replay; the backtest's `EVAL_SEASON_SIM`, which starts from its walk-forward ratings, puts a 92-point champion at 14.1%. Do not read title odds as a precise prediction of the winning points total. `examples/season_eval.rs` measures the same shortfall across every season (see "Season-level evaluation").

## Calibration (`tests/calibration.rs`)

The model that ships is checked against the real league: the default 0.5/0.3/0.2 blend, built as `main.rs` builds it, at preseason with nothing played (`shipped_preseason_world()`), so the check is of the model, not of how far the season has got. It is measured per fixture (the exact `fixture_probs` split and expected goals, averaged over the 306 fixtures) and per simulated season (`World::simulate`, 10 batches of 200 seasons, seed 4242, which also draws `RATING_SIGMA`), against the 4,588 played matches since 2012-13 (walkovers dropped). A history figure's standard error is the larger of the per-match and the season-clustered one: scoring moves from season to season (2.64 to 3.05 goals a match), so the clustered error for goals is 0.033, not 0.025. Measured (`cargo test --test calibration -- --nocapture` prints the report; these are not in a metrics file):
- home wins 44.9% per fixture against 45.4%, inside the history's error (0.7 points);
- draws under-predicted: 24.8% per fixture and 24.4% per season against 25.9%, 1.1 and 1.4 points short (history SE 0.65; z −1.6 and −2.2);
- away wins over-predicted: 30.4% against 28.8% (z +2.4), where the missing draws go;
- goals over-predicted: 2.878 a match per fixture and 2.915 per season against 2.787 (z +2.7 and +3.8); home 1.627 against 1.568, away 1.251 against 1.219.

Two guards. `calibration_is_pinned` holds today's figures in `LIMITS`: home and draw rates within 1 point and goals within 0.05 a match, so a 2-point shift fails. `calibration_stays_within_its_band_of_the_history` keeps each gap inside its band: home wins ±1.5 points, draws ±1.5 per fixture and ±2 per season, goals ±0.15 per fixture and ±0.2 per season, ending about half a point (0.06-0.07 goals) past where the known draw and goal gaps stand, because they are errors, not slack to spend. Away wins and goals by side are reported only, and mean points per club are checked against the identity 17 × (3 − draw rate) instead of pinned. A deliberate model change (constants, a Dixon-Coles refit, new history) re-measures, re-pins `LIMITS` and updates these figures and the READMEs'. Phase 4 left them as they were (re-run 2026-10-19): it changed only overrides and pins, which the check uses neither of. A pure-Elo `World` is not checked: `ENSEMBLE_WEIGHTS=1,0,0` is an opt-out, not what runs.
